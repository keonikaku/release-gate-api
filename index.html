The responses this suite proves

A suite that only shows the happy path proves the service works when nothing goes wrong, which is the easy half. Counts are cases across the whole suite, not just the walkthrough.

201
3

Created. The change was recorded and the service says where it is.

200
17

Accepted. The gate ran the rules, or the lifecycle move was legal.

400
3

Refused. The request was read and understood, and a rule said no.

404
4

No such change. The identifier does not name anything.

409
4

Conflict. The request is legal in general and not from this state.

422
4

Unreadable. The request could not be parsed into a change at all.

500
1

The service failed. It says so rather than reporting success.

400 and 422 are not the same refusal, and this service does not treat them as one. A 422 means the request could not be read: a missing field, a timestamp with no timezone, a state that does not exist. A 400 means the request was read and understood, and a rule refused it. Collapsing them is common and it costs something real: in any log, metric or alert built on status codes, a caller with a bug and a change that is not ready become the same event. The reasoning is recorded in decision 0002, and there are 3 cases on one and 4 on the other.
