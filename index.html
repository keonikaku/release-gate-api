The responses this suite proves

A suite that only shows the happy path proves the service works when nothing goes wrong, which is the easy half. These are the ways a request can be refused, each with cases behind it.

201 responses
3

Created. The change was recorded and the service says where it is.

200 responses
17

Accepted. The gate ran the rules, or the lifecycle move was legal.

400 responses
2

Refused. The request was read and understood, and a rule said no.

404 responses
4

No such change. The identifier does not name anything.

409 responses
4

Conflict. The request is legal in general and not from this state.

422 responses
4

Unreadable. The request could not be parsed into a change at all.

500 responses
1

The service failed. It says so rather than reporting success.

400 and 422 are not the same refusal, and this service does not treat them as one. A 422 means the request could not be read: a missing field, a timestamp with no timezone, a state that does not exist. A 400 means the request was read and understood, and a rule refused it. Collapsing them is common and it costs something real: in any log, metric or alert built on status codes, a caller with a bug and a change that is not ready become the same event. The reasoning is recorded in decision 0002, and there are 2 cases on one and 4 on the other.

The cases

Grouped by the status each one expects. Expected is declared in the test itself. Returned is what the service answered on this run. The build fails if those two disagree, so the column on the right is a result rather than a restatement of the column on the left.
